# Seam corridors and fenced character transfer

Status: accepted; implemented by parts 1, 2, 3a, 3b and 3c of #145 (owner decision Q5 = A: three sequential PRs; part 3 split into 3a, 3b and 3c with owner approval).

## Boundary

A seam between two logical zones is explicit content. Each side binds a `HandoffCorridor` (`ZoneContent::with_handoff_corridors`): the neighbouring `ZoneId`, an interaction-light XZ rectangle, a crossing strip inside it and the coordinate offset into the neighbour. Corridors are part of the content identity; content without corridors keeps its revision.

Inside a corridor, movement, facing and presentation continue, but nothing irreversible or economically meaningful begins: loot claims, vendor trades and quest accept/turn-in/abandon are refused with error code 35 (`HandoffCorridor`), and creatures evade the player's attacks, so no tap, kill credit, loot or boss reward starts there. Creatures never aggro a player in a corridor, and an engaged creature leashes home when its target stands in one or its body comes within `CORRIDOR_TURN_BACK_UNITS` (2 m) of one. Content validation keeps every creature's wander area, NPC, routine path, the graveyard and every player spawn slot clear of corridors (creatures and NPCs also of the margin), so no unit crosses a seam and no combat spans two zones.

## Transfer contract

Walking and teleporting share one contract, `CharacterTransfer`, identified by a `TransferId` independent of sessions and player IDs. `ZoneSimulation::departures` reports players in a crossing strip and travellers to a stone of another zone, with where each continues.

1. **Freeze** (source, the cutover): the player leaves the live simulation and the source exports the durable `CharacterRecord` located in the destination's coordinates, the held movement and body velocity, and the last applied command sequence. Refused while climbing, rolling, or with this tick's reward grants not yet committed (TECH-005).
2. **Stage** (destination): validated against the destination content and held, not live. Idempotent per transfer ID; a different transfer under the same ID, or a second transfer of a staged character, is refused.
3. **Activate** (destination): the character becomes live and only commands after the cutover sequence apply, so stale and duplicate sequences fail closed. Repeated activation as the same player is a no-op.
4. **Retire** (source) once the destination accepted, or **abort** (source): the frozen player returns live and unchanged.

The zone holds at most one live copy of a character and never decides which side wins. The control plane's fenced handoff record does (part 2): a host activates only after the destination's acceptance is recorded and retires the source only after that. Frozen and staged transfers are host-phase state and are not canonical; recovery reconciles them from the handoff record and the character store. Session and tick-local state (target, queued intents, casts, auras, guard, combo, swing and roll timers) ends at the cutover, as at logout. No general action replay is introduced.

## Host-to-host transfer (part 2)

Three parties take part and none decides alone: each zone holds the live copy, the character store (`mmorpg-characters`) holds the lease and the in-flight transfer, and the control plane's fenced `HandoffRegistry` record decides the outcome. `mmorpg_game_server::handoff` composes them; every step is idempotent per transfer ID.

1. **Begin** (source): freeze, then `CharacterStore::begin_handoff` stores the frozen transfer under the source's lease, then the registry prepares the ticket. While a handoff is pending nobody commits, releases or acquires the character, not even a newer epoch of the source zone. A refused step undoes the earlier ones.
2. **Accept** (destination): stage, then record acceptance. **`Accepted` is the decision point**: before it the source may `abort` (a new `Aborted` phase) and stays authoritative; after it the transfer only completes. Then `complete_handoff` commits the transfer's record and grants the destination the lease in one atomic store step; the old lease is fenced at the store.
3. **Retire** (source): `commit` in the registry, then drop the frozen copy.
4. **Activate** (destination): only once the source committed, or the transfer is accepted and the source's zone lease is no longer current (a fenced host cannot restore an accepted transfer).

Recovery, at every phase:

- Not yet accepted (no record, or `Prepared`): the source aborts in the registry, cancels the store's pending handoff and unfreezes its player. Abort and acceptance race in the registry and exactly one wins; a late offer to the destination is refused and leaves nothing staged.
- Accepted or committed: the source retires; the destination completes the lease move and activates.
- Both hosts' host-phase state lost: the store still holds the pending transfer. Whoever next acquires the character runs `resolve_pending_handoff`: an accepted transfer completes to its destination; an unaccepted one is cancelled only by a newer epoch of the source zone, which proves the host that began it is fenced and can no longer prepare or accept it. The character then returns from the last committed record (source) or the transfer's record (destination), and a newer epoch supersedes the lease as for any crashed zone.

The registry's `commit` and `abort` accept the ticket's lease or a newer epoch of the same zone, which speaks for its fenced predecessor. `commit` no longer requires the destination's lease to be current: the source confirming its own retirement does not depend on the destination host.

Hosts reach each other over the `transfer_link` TCP link: length-prefixed offer and activate requests, each answered ok or refused. It is unauthenticated and unencrypted, for development and trusted LAN only (Q1 = A, ADR 0003); production transfer credentials are not claimed.

Formats: `mmorpg-protocol` encodes `CharacterTransfer` (`CHARACTER_TRANSFER_WIRE_VERSION`, a random token). The character entry gains the pending handoff and the last completed handoff with its lease generation; `ENTRY_FORMAT_VERSION` became a random token, so older entries are refused (no migration, pre-1.0).

## Test zones and waystones (part 3a)

Owner decision Q2 (revised): the live handoff is proven between two small dedicated logical test zones, Seam Test West (zone 901) and Seam Test East (902) (`mmorpg_core::seam_test`). They share one flat layout; East coordinates are West's shifted 100 m toward −X, and each crossing strip lands a walker inside the other side's corridor but short of its crossing, so nobody is handed straight back. Greyhaven Vale keeps its geometry and has no seam into them; its outpost waystone reaches them by teleport only. Hosts pick content by zone ID (`content_for_zone`): 901 and 902 are the test zones, every other ID Greyhaven.

Waystones (Q4 = A) form one global network (`mmorpg_core::WAYSTONES`): a stable ID, name, logical zone, stone and arrival point per stone; content binds the stones that stand in it. A living player within 8 m of a bound stone unlocks it for good. The unlocks are a durable character field, canonical player state and six spare bits of the projection's self flags, which caps the network at six stones until a wire change grows it. From within 8 m of a stone, travel to an unlocked stone is instant, with no cast or cooldown, and is refused in combat (error 36), in a corridor (35) and toward a locked, unknown or current stone (37). Travel within a zone moves the player there. Travel to a stone of another zone uses the same fenced transfer as walking (part 3b).

Formats: command tag 30 (`UseWaystone`), canonical intent 23, error codes 36 and 37, a new protocol ID, and a new random `CHARACTER_RECORD_SCHEMA_VERSION` token: stored characters of the old schema are refused, with no migration.

## Shared control plane and live hosts (part 3b)

**One authority trait.** `mmorpg_control_plane::HandoffAuthority` holds the zone-lease checks and handoff records one host step needs (`ensure_current`, `current_lease`, `record`, `prepare`, `accept`, `commit`, `abort`). `ReferenceAuthority` wraps the in-memory `ZoneDirectory` and `HandoffRegistry` for tests and scenarios. `postgres::PostgresHandoffs` keeps the records in `mmorpg_coordination.handoffs` (directory migration 2), one row per transfer with a partial unique index allowing one unsettled transfer per entity. Each step is one transaction. It first share-locks the zone grants it names through `lock_current_writer`, so no grant changes between the check and the record write, then applies the reference model's rules. `mmorpg_game_server::handoff` is generic over the authority and over a `TransferZone`: a `ZoneSimulation` directly, or a live zone through its host inputs.

**Live zones.** Every tick a zone publishes its departures to the host's handoff service through a `HandoffChannel`. A departure is a seam crossing, or travel to a stone of another zone (`UseWaystone` no longer refuses it on a zone whose host attached a handoff service, `enable_waystone_departures`). Changes to canonical state are trusted host inputs, so replay sees them in order with commands: freeze toward the player's current departure, put back, arrive (stage and activate for the claiming session), and decline. Retiring the frozen copy and the redirect are host-phase state that the channel hands over. A taken-back or declined player is not offered again until it leaves the crossing strip. A taken-back waystone departure is dropped.

**The service** (`handoff_host`) runs off the tick under the host's character-storage lock, so periodic commits never see a half-moved character. The source freezes, records the frozen transfer with the lease (`begin_outbound`) and offers it over the transfer link. Once the destination accepted, it commits and retires. The departed session's projection becomes a redirect notice to the destination's session route ([PROTOCOL.md](../PROTOCOL.md#redirect-notice-145)), carrying the cutover sequence. A refused or unreachable destination is reconciled from the record: the player goes back live unless the destination accepted after all. The destination validates an offer against its own content in a scratch zone, accepts it and takes over the lease. The character goes live only when its client connects and claims it there, and only once the source retired (`source_retired`). A claim that finds a pending handoff in the store resolves it first (`resolve_pending_handoff`).

**Composition.** `MMORPG_HANDOFF_DATABASE_URL` selects the trusted-LAN handoff host (Q1 = A). It keeps the characters (`mmorpg_characters`), zone grants and handoff records in one PostgreSQL database shared with its peers, and migrates both schemas at start. It takes its zones' grants: a first grant, one after a retired owner, or its own grant from before a restart under the same `MMORPG_HOST_ID`, which it retires itself. Another host's live grant is refused. It renews the grants every third of `MMORPG_GRANT_TTL_MS` and stops when a renewal fails. Leases use each zone's grant epoch. Zone state starts fresh each run; characters come from the store. `MMORPG_TRANSFER_LINK` is this host's link address. `MMORPG_ZONE_ROUTES` (`zone=host:port@session-route,…`) names each peer zone's link and the route its clients use, a static stand-in for TECH-007's routing API. The file-mode development host is unchanged.

**Quest logs** belong to the character. Content can carry other zones' quests for log validation (`ZoneContent::with_carried_quests`), which is part of its identity. The Seam Test zones carry Greyhaven's, so a log crosses unchanged and its quests make no progress there.

## Client cutover (part 3c)

**Following a redirect.** A client changes host only on the redirect notice, which comes after the source committed the handoff (TECH-007), never on staging or acceptance. Both clients check that the notice comes from their current zone for their claimed character, that it leads to another zone, that its transfer is not one they already followed, and that its route names the destination zone (`…/matches/zone-<destination>`). Then they close the source's connection and forget its route and resume token, so the old route is never used again: a later interruption resumes at the destination only. At the destination they claim the same character, repeating the claim while the destination refuses it (it does until the source retired the character), and keep their command sequence, so the destination applies only commands after the cutover sequence. Intent made while travelling is dropped, as during a resume; the character arrives stopped. A destination that does not take the character within 20 s fails the session closed; nothing falls back to an anonymous player or back to the source. The native client does this in `ClientSession::follow`, the browser in `OnlineZoneSource` (a new `travelling` link state).

**Per-zone presentation.** `mmorpg_scenery::scenery_for_zone` gives each zone's scenery, as `content_for_zone` gives its content. The Seam Test zones are flat, with their waystone and palisades along their walls. Across the seam each shows the neighbour's landscape (its stone and walls, in this zone's coordinates) and none of its units (Q3 = A), so the wall that ends a zone behind its seam is not drawn. Greyhaven's authored kits (the Outpost buildings, the Sunken Shrine, the walk-in buildings and the ambient animals) need Greyhaven's props and draw only on Greyhaven's scenery (`Scenery::greyhaven`). The native window swaps the scenery, content, minimap and presentation when projections of another zone arrive. The browser's WASM module presents one zone at a time (`presentZone`, Greyhaven until an online session goes elsewhere); the page reloads its view for the new zone, and the local world switches back to Greyhaven on entry. The local zone host never reads the presentation.

**The journey.** `scripts/smoke-handoff.py` starts PostgreSQL 17 in Docker, two handoff hosts for Seam Test West and East, and the native client's headless `--autopilot`: the character unlocks West's stone, walks through the seam to East, unlocks East's stone and teleports back to West, each time following the redirect to the other host.

## Not covered here

Hosts without a handoff service (the browser's local host, the file-mode development host and the journaled PostgreSQL host) keep refusing travel to another zone's stone with error 37. Production admission, transfer credentials and routing remain TECH-007's open items, and terminal handoff records have no retention policy yet. Without TECH-007's routing API a new entry still starts at the route the client was configured with, not at the zone holding the character. Peer hosts share one certificate trust (the native client pins one certificate hash for every route it follows).

# Greyhaven Vale starter zone

Target: a playable tech demo comparable in *shape* to a classic MMO starter area. A new level-1 character leaves the Greyhaven Outpost hub, fights creatures in surrounding subzones, completes a short quest chain, gains levels and gear, and meets a named boss. Every gameplay outcome stays server-authoritative, deterministic and replay/recovery-complete.

This document is the design contract for that slice. The implementation plan below is tracked as GitHub issues. Each step lands as its own pull request.

The browser demo runs the same `ZoneSimulation` through a WASM build as a local, single-player zone host ([ADR 0002](adr/0002-browser-embeds-zone-simulation.md)). The browser presentation consumes the same player-scoped wire projections that the network host sends. It never reads canonical state.

## Invariants every step keeps

- One writer per zone epoch; fencing, handoff and interest contracts are unchanged.
- Gameplay failures are **outcomes**, not transport errors. A command that is well-formed but not currently allowed, such as an ability on cooldown or an out-of-range target, is accepted by `apply_command`, produces a player-visible feedback event, and never closes the session. Only malformed payloads return errors.
- Discrete intents such as casting, looting or accepting quests are processed during the tick in `(player_id, sequence)` order. They are never applied inside `apply_command` against a partially advanced world.
- All authoritative state enters the canonical snapshot: units, auras, cooldowns, AI state, spawn timers, corpses, loot, inventories, quest progress and the zone RNG state. Recovery at any tick must reproduce the continuation exactly (tested per step).
- Iteration over units is ordered (`BTreeMap` or sorted `Vec`). No `HashMap` iteration, wall clock or floating point decides authoritative state. Integer trigonometry uses a const-generated table.
- Snapshot projections are bounded: a deterministic priority cap on entity count plus a byte budget test. Events per snapshot are bounded, and losing a datagram may drop cosmetic events only. Durable facts such as quest progress, inventory and XP also live in periodically re-sent state.
- Content (collision, creature templates, items, quests, abilities) is identified by the zone content revision. Changing it requires a new revision.

## Coordinates and scale

- 100 simulation units per metre, Y up, 60 Hz ticks.
- The playable square is about 416 m × 416 m, centred on the origin (#159: the original 240 m valley plus a ring of four outer regions — Greyfang Ridge, Southmeadow Downs, Hollowpine Reach and Eastwater Fen — joined by the Vale Ring Road, the Fen Track and the Downs Trail). Invisible boundary walls enclose it; mountains outside are scenery.
- **Walkable ground** is physically flat (y = 0) across the original 240 m valley. The outer valley (#159, content revision 13) has physical hills up to 10 m: an authored 4 m heightfield (`greyhaven_vale::terrain()`) that the zone's Rapier world walks units on, with flat cells left to the floor half-space. Structures and trunks out there stand on it, creatures spawn on it. Clients draw the terrain as physical ground plus the presentation relief (at most about 0.6 m) and draw units at their physics position plus that relief.
- **Village basin, slice 1** (#423, content revision 27, Atlas 5.1.84, `greyhaven_vale::basin`): Millbrook Farm stands on a terrace 11 m above the Outpost's valley floor. Its western, northern and southern edges are cliffs (11 m over one 4 m cell, 70°) that no unit walks up; to the east it falls gently (17°) into the Fen hills, where the Fen Track leaves it. The **Millbrook Climb** (Millbrook Road, about 1:5) runs from the east gate north along a ledge in the cliff face, round a hairpin landing 6.4 m up and south up a cutting onto the terrace. The **Millbrook Steps** (about 1:3.6) lead from the farmyard south down a second ledge to the valley floor and the South Road. Farm structures, the mill dock, ramps and hoist, creatures, props and Farmer Osric all stand on the terrace. Clients draw faces steeper than 45° as bare rock. Redbrand Hollow and Wolfrun Woods terraces come in slice 3.
- **Village basin, slice 2** (#423, content revision 28): Stillwater Lake and the Sunken Shrine lie in the same 11 m terrace. Its west cliff faces the Hollow Road, its north cliff Redbrand Hollow and its south cliff the basin's northern apron; east of the farm's west cliff it runs on into the farm terrace, and the eastern slope into the Fen hills now starts beyond the shrine's cave (x 128 m). The water surface, the lake basin, the shrine plaza, the flooded cave and its air-pocket room all moved up 11 m with it. The **Lakeshore Climb** (Lakeshore Path, about 1:6 then 1:4.5) leaves the Hollow Road north of the gate, runs north along a ledge in the west cliff to a landing 5.6 m up and back south up a cutting onto the lake's west shore. The **Stillwater Rim Trail** joins the farmyard to the lake's south shore across the terrace, a loop that skips the Outpost.
- **Village basin, slice 3** (#423): the rim closes round the basin. Everything in the inner valley outside the basin (the Outpost and its apron, about x ±44 m and z −24 m to +96 m, plus a notch west of the lake up to Redbrand Hollow's cliff at z −64 m) now stands on the 11 m rim, and beyond 128 m from the Outpost the rim descends gently (17°) into the outer hills. Wolfrun Woods, Redbrand Hollow with its camp, watch post and powder barrels, and the Greyfang cave (whose floor, walls, roof and sealed threshold rose with it) are on the rim. Three more climbs are cut into the cliffs: the **Wolfrun Climb** (Wolfrun Trail), the Millbrook Climb's mirror image west of the Outpost; the **Hollow Climb** (Hollow Road), west along a ledge under the Hollow's cliff to a landing 6.4 m up and back east up a cutting into the camp; and the **South Climb** (South Road), its mirror image over the southern cliff. The **Wolfrun Ridge Path** joins the woods to the Hollow along the rim, a loop that skips the Outpost, and the Downs Trail now leaves from the top of the Wolfrun Climb. The Hollow's western cliffs stand back to z −80 m for the ridge path.
- **Farmland and Hamlets** (#424, Atlas 5.1.85–5.1.86, `greyhaven_vale::farmland`): the first major region beyond the Vale, in the same zone, south of it behind a mountain ridge (24–36 m, every face steeper than 45°) that replaces the Vale's southern wall. The South Road crosses the Vale Ring Road and climbs a cliff notch through the ridge: a cutting into its north face, a gorge east along its crest and a cutting south onto a plateau 8 m above the Vale's floor. The plateau (x ±208 m, z +252 m to +640 m, about the Vale's area) rolls gently between level hamlets and fields. The Farmland Road runs south past eleven fenced, ploughed fields to three hamlets of houses, farmhouses, barns, wells and windmills (the West, East and South Hamlet Lanes), and a keep-style tower 18 m tall stands beside the road halfway down as the landmark. One named area covers it; it has no creatures, NPCs or quests yet. Wire positions are full `i32` units ([PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md#full-i32-positions-424)).
- **Bandit Highlands** (#425, Atlas 5.1.85–5.1.86 and 5.1.i425, `greyhaven_vale::highlands`): the second major region beyond the Vale, in the same zone, north of it across from the farmland, above an escarpment (40–52 m, every face steeper than 45°) that replaces the Vale's northern wall behind Greyfang Ridge. The **Highland Pass** leaves the Vale Ring Road at x +52 m and winds up the escarpment: a cutting into its southern face, a ledge climbing east (about 1:5), a hairpin landing, a ledge climbing back west and a gorge north onto broken high ground 24 m above the Vale's floor (x ±208 m, z −276 m to −632 m, about the Vale's area), whose swells and knolls rise up to 8 m more. Eighteen rock crags (8–16 m) and scattered pines stand across it. The **Highland Trail** winds north to the landmark, a palisaded bandit stronghold (80 m × 64 m yard) round a keep-style tower 20 m tall, with tents, a campfire and stores; the **Lookout Path** branches east to a 14 m lookout tower on a level knoll. One named area covers it; it has no creatures, NPCs or quests yet. Redbrand Hollow, Greyfang Ridge and the Greyfang cave keep their routes.
- **Marsh-Lake Wetlands** (#426, Atlas 5.1.85–5.1.86 and 5.1.i426, `greyhaven_vale::marsh`): the third major region beyond the Vale, in the same zone, east of it beyond Eastwater Fen and Stillwater Lake, behind a mountain ridge (24–36 m, every face steeper than 45°) that replaces the Vale's eastern wall. The **Marsh Pass** leaves the Vale Ring Road at z +40 m and winds through the ridge: a cutting into its western face, a gorge climbing north along its crest, a level gorge east across it, a gorge falling back south and a cutting east down onto wetlands 4 m above the Vale's floor (x +252 m to +632 m, z ±208 m, about the Vale's area). Low hummocks surround five real water volumes, each ringed with reeds: the 200 m × 160 m **Marsh Lake** (6 m deep at its middle) and four pools, two deep enough to swim. The **Marsh Trail** leads to the lake, where a raised **Causeway** crosses the water to the landmark, a drowned watchtower 18 m tall on an island ringed by standing stones; the **Reed Path** wades through the shallow Reed Ford to the southern pool and the **Heron Path** runs past a ruined cottage to the northern one. One named area covers it; it has no creatures, NPCs or quests yet. The walls now enclose the column of highlands, Vale and farmland plus the wetlands; the corners of the rectangle round them are mountains. Stillwater Lake, the Sunken Shrine and Eastwater Fen stay as they were.
- **Climbing and mantling** (#490, Atlas 5.1.40, 5.1.i490 and 5.9.i490, `mmorpg_core::climb`, `greyhaven_vale::climbing`): jump at a climbable face to hold it, press forward and back to climb, strafe along it and jump to let go; climbing is slow (0.9 m/s) with no stamina or height limit. The Outpost and stronghold palisades, the fences, crags, boulders, shore rocks, the Redbrand cliffs and the Vale basin's cliffs climb, with sheer exceptions (the Hollow's back wall, two highland crags, the stronghold's northern palisade and the cliff under Stillwater Lake); buildings and towers do not, but ladders lead up the Eastwater watchtower, the highland lookout and the drowned watchtower. The highland escarpment, the farmland ridge and the wetlands ridge each have one hard climbing route beside their pass (8 m wide: x +120 to +128 m on the escarpment, x −24 to −16 m on the farmland ridge, z +64 to +72 m on the wetlands ridge) and are sheer elsewhere. Any ledge within reach (2.2 m above the feet, about 3.4 m with a jump) can be mantled without marking: jump at it, or reach it in the air pressing forward, or top out a climb.
- **Projectiles** (#286, Atlas 5.2.71): Ranger shots (Aimed Shot, Serpent Sting, Concussive Shot) fire arrows that track their target in a ballistic arc (54 m/s) and apply the ability when they arrive; Aimed Shot without a target flies freely along the facing and hits whatever it meets. The three Greyfang outriders are Redbrand Crossbowmen (levels 3–5) whose Crossbow Bolt (1 s wind-up, 8 s cooldown, 20 m, 5–8 damage) is a tracking bolt. Walls, props, terrain and other units in the way stop a projectile, and a Warden's raised shield turned toward it blocks it; spent projectiles stay stuck for 3 s (1 s in a unit). Both clients draw placeholder shafts until asset-tooling's bow/arrow bundle lands.
- **Breakable props** (#288, content revision 15, Atlas 5.2.76): crates and barrels by Greyhaven's smithy and houses, crates, barrels and three fence sections at Millbrook Farm, and five Blasting Powder Barrels in the Redbrand camp (spawns 900–924). They are targetable level-1 units (crate 12, barrel 15, fence 18, powder barrel 10 health) that Tab, clicks, swings, abilities, projectiles and blasts all reach; they never move or fight, give no experience and drop nothing. A broken prop vanishes into short-lived Rapier debris (dynamic boxes that fly, settle and despawn after 5 s) and stands again after 5 min once no unit stands on its spot. A powder barrel explodes as it breaks: 12–16 damage and a 6 m knockback within 5 m, both falling off to half at the edge and shielded by walls, to every player, creature and prop it reaches, so neighbouring barrels go up too. Both clients draw props and debris as placeholder boxes from the shared unit presenter.
- **Greyfang cave** (#159, content revision 14, `greyhaven_vale::cave::GREYFANG`): an enclosed rock chamber set into the Greyfang hillside east of Redbrand Hollow (x 44–84 m, z −148 to −128 m). A 4 m mouth in its south wall opens on the flat foot of the ridge; inside, a 20 m × 12 m outer cavern joins an 8 m × 12 m inner chamber through a 4 m passage. Rock walls (4.5 m) and a 1 m roof slab are fixed boxes; the terrain is flattened under the floor. Both clients cut the roof away while the player stands under it, like the inn. The `Cave` data is reusable. Since content revision 25 it is Redbrand Hollow's shared outer cavern (#160, below).
- Yaw is a `u16` angle (65 536 = one turn). Yaw 0 faces +Z and increasing yaw turns toward +X: `direction = (sin yaw, cos yaw)`.

Content revision 17 adds a **large creature** (#297): the Hill Ogre squats east of the ruined Southmeadow farmstead. A creature at least 1.4 m across is large: its capsule body-blocks smaller units like any body; it holds its ground while idle (and so draws nothing from the zone RNG, leaving every other creature's script unchanged); a step that would carry it into a smaller living unit shoves that unit 1.2 m aside, off its path, as a forced move with the usual immunity (Atlas 5.2.72; walls stop it); and it casts **Stomp** (ability 18: 1 s wind-up, 12 s cooldown) once its target is within 3.5 m, hurting and knocking back every player within 3.5 m it can see, falling off to half at the edge.

Content revision 18 adds the **mill hoist** (#56, `greyhaven_vale::MILL_HOIST`), a moving platform: a 2 m square stone pillar against the Millbrook windmill's south face. It rests 5 s with its top flush with the ground, rises 8 m to the windmill's flat top (its gallery) at 1.5 m/s, rests 5 s and sinks back, forever; its position is a pure function of the zone tick, so canonical snapshots and projections carry nothing new and both clients draw it from content and the projection's tick. Its box fills the shaft under its top, so it sweeps no space a unit could stand in except on it. Physics carries every unit standing on it that is not jumping; a rider walks off onto the gallery at the top, or off the edge and falls.

Content revision 19 adds the first **ramps** (#59, `greyhaven_vale::MILL_RAMPS`): a 1.5 m stone loading dock west of the windmill with a shallow 17° cart ramp up its west side, which units walk up and down grounded on the slope, and a steep 60° chute on its north side that no unit can climb (both controllers climb at most 45°). A ramp is a wedge filling the lower half of its box, its top rising toward one horizontal direction; it is solid physics on both backends, and both clients draw it from the same content.

Content revision 20 adds the first **destructible structure** (#305, `greyhaven_vale::watch_post`): the Redbrand watch post at the camp's southern edge, east of the Hollow Road: two timber walls (120 health each) and the lookout deck resting on both (60). Fire Blasts and powder barrels hurt every piece they reach and see (their top roll, with falloff), arrows and bolts chip a piece by 3; a piece passes through damaged, crumbling and destroyed stages, a destroyed wall's collider leaves the world (the west wall leaves a knee-high rubble blocker) and the deck falls once neither wall stands. Pieces regrow 5 minutes after their last change once their spot is clear; Interact (T) near a damaged piece repairs it for free, five times faster, until the player moves (Atlas 5.2.80).

Content revision 21 adds the first **movable props** (#58, Atlas 5.2.82): two Heavy Crates (30 health, spawns 917 and 918) in Millbrook Farm's yard. Shield Bash, Charge, blasts, stomps and shoves slide a heavy crate instead of knocking it back: it slides upright on Rapier under gravity and friction about as far as the knockback would have carried a unit, stops at walls, other props and units, and comes to rest; units meet it as a fixed box wherever it stands. Its exact position and velocity live in the canonical snapshot, so a restored zone continues a slide exactly. Broken, it shatters like a crate and stands again at its spawn, at rest.

Creatures **navigate around blockers** (#307): a creature chasing a target or running home whose capsule, swept straight above knee height, would meet a wall, a structure piece, rubble or a closed door walks an A* route on a 1 m grid instead (cells tested by physics overlap queries, cached until a door moves or a piece breaks or regrows), steering at the farthest of the next six route cells it can reach straight. So a creature boxed in by pieces comes out once a wall breaks, and goes around a broken post's rubble. Spell and arrow sight lines follow the pieces that stand, since a broken piece's collider leaves the world.

## Systems

### Movement

- Command `Move { forward: -1..=1, strafe: -1..=1, facing: u16 }`. The server rotates local intent by `facing` through the integer trig table. Run speed is 21 units/tick (6.3 m/s); a backward component uses 13 units/tick. Facing is intent and has no collision effect.
- Command `Jump`: takes effect only when grounded. A thin overlap query just below the feet decides that; touching any other body (geometry or another unit) counts, side contact with a wall does not. Vertical velocity is set to a constant, and physics integrates gravity. The next tick consumes the intent either way, so an airborne jump is ignored rather than buffered.
- Moving interrupts casts. Dead units cannot move.

### Units and combat

- A unit is a player, creature or NPC. `EntityRef = Player(u32) | Creature(u32) | Npc(u32)` on the wire as `kind: u8, id: u32`. Physics body IDs occupy disjoint namespaces per kind.
- Unit state: level, health/max, resource kind/value/max, faction, target, cast state, auras (bounded), combat flag, death state.
- Players: `SelectTarget(EntityRef | none)`, `StartAutoAttack`, `StopAttack`, `UseAbility { ability, target }`. The server validates range, target and resource.
- Auto-attack uses weapon swing timers. Damage is `base + level scaling` with deterministic variance from the zone RNG. Crits apply ×1.5.
- Death: creatures leave a lootable corpse. Players drop to a dead state and `ReleaseSpirit` respawns them at the graveyard with half health. Health and resource regenerate out of combat.

### Classes (level 1–10)

There is a 1.5 s global cooldown. Casts have a cast time, and moving or being stunned interrupts them. Auras have tick-precise durations and periodic effects.

Step 8a implemented the kit in core (abilities 1–12 of the catalog; lurkers cast Muck Bolt, 13, and bandits Crude Bandage, 14, in content revision 6). Its numbers are in `mmorpg_core::ABILITY_CATALOG` and the rules in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md#units-combat-and-creature-ai). Recorded decisions:

- Cleave hits the target and up to two more living creatures within 3 m of the target in `EntityRef` order, for weapon damage each, instead of a frontal arc.
- "Per level" amounts scale with the caster's levels above 1, like the player damage curve; weapon damage is one draw from the player's melee range. Ability damage has no miss or critical roll.
- Rage comes from auto-attack hits dealt and from every creature hit taken (swing or ability), even a fully absorbed one; death drains it.
- Durations, cooldowns, the global cooldown and the five-second rule count the tick they start in; damage and heal over time pulse every period from there.
- A cast completes against its target without a second range check; a target that died ends it as interrupted. Targeted ranged abilities need line of sight at use and again at completion: a deterministic physics ray between body centres must not hit static content colliders (units never block), otherwise the new `LineOfSight` error refuses it at no cost. Static content means every body in the static collider namespace: content boxes, door leaves, ramps (#59) and the mill hoist (#56); a 2-unit plank blocks, a gap lets the line through, and equally near hits are broken by body ID on both physics backends (#57). The browser's WASM build reaches the same sight decisions and projections as the native build (`web/tests/sight-parity.test.ts` runs the native session of `crates/mmorpg-wasm/tests/sight_parity.rs` and compares its digest live with the WASM digest), except broken props' debris: Rapier's dynamic pieces are not bit-identical across wasm32 and native. A Warden's **Charge** also needs a path as wide as its body (#57, Atlas 5.2.79): the Warden's capsule, raised to knee height (32 units, so ground, slopes, kerbs and the campfire do not count), swept toward the target until melee reach must meet no static geometry, or the charge is refused with `LineOfSight` at no cost; a sight line threading a gap narrower than the Warden is not enough. A creature's Muck Bolt neither starts nor lands without line of sight (a broken line fizzles as interrupted). A creature's cast at a player who dies or leaves ends silently.
- Invalid or repeated class choices report the new `InvalidClass` error.
- Heroic Strike also has a **directed mode** (#154, Atlas 5.2.27/5.2.53/5.2.55): the same ability, unlock, Rage cost, cooldown and global cooldown, executed by a `DirectedStrike` command with a facing and a left/centre/right, low/middle/high aim. Core resolves the sweep's physical coverage (a ±30° arc turned 45° toward the side, melee reach, the physics body's overlap with the height band, static-geometry line of sight) and strikes the nearest covered creature for the weapon-strike damage; a sweep covering nobody whiffs but still pays. A high slash passes over a wolf; a low one reaches a rat. See [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md#commands-wire-version-11).
- A Warden can hold a **directional shield guard** (#156, Atlas 5.2.29–5.2.68) with a `Guard` command naming the low, middle or high lane. The guard protects only while the player is a living, unstunned Warden standing on ground (the jump probe; a jump or fall suspends it, Atlas 5.2.63) and not recovering from a break. A creature melee hit that rolled damage meets a protecting guard when its lane matches and it comes from the shield's coverage (Atlas 5.2.68): the lane is the height band containing the attacker's contact point, three quarters up its physics body, above the defender's feet (rats low, wolves and boars middle, humanoids high), and the bearing from the defender to the attacker must lie within ±75° of the facing turned 30° toward the shield (left) side, so the front and shield flank are covered and the sword flank and rear are not. Its impact is the rolled damage plus the attacker's physics velocity toward the defender (units per tick) times its body volume over the defender's. Below 18 the blow is fully blocked (no damage, no Rage); at 18 or more it overpowers: the guard breaks for 45 ticks (1.5 s) and the blow lands in full (Atlas 5.2.33/5.2.35). Stationary blows of ordinary creatures are blocked; a charging wolf, boar or humanoid and the heaviest critical hits overpower. The hold survives a break, so the same lane protects again when recovery ends (Atlas 5.2.59). A protecting guard slows horizontal movement to 60 % (Atlas 5.2.61); a suspended or broken one does not. Guarding costs nothing (Atlas 5.2.66). Death and a directed strike (the last-pressed gesture, Atlas 5.2.37) lower the guard; only melee swings are blocked, never abilities. A fully blocked blow grants no Rage, unlike an absorbed one. See [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md#commands-wire-version-11).
- Class-specific health and damage, Ranger Auto Shot, Garrick's abilities, the action bar and cast bars are out of this step (8b and later).

### Creature AI

- States: `Idle` (wander within a radius using the zone RNG), `Engaged` (chase to melee or cast range, attack on the swing timer), `Evading` (leash beyond 40 m from spawn or no valid threat: run home, reset to full health, ignore damage), `Dead` (corpse until timeout, then a respawn timer; claiming rewards does not shorten this schedule).
- Aggro radius is 10 m ± 1 m per level difference, clamped. Nearby same-family creatures assist when one is engaged.
- The threat table is ordered by `(threat desc, entity asc)`.
- Steering is direct toward the target. Physics blocks obstacles; no navmesh in this slice.

### Progression, loot and economy

- Experience curve for levels 1–10. Kill XP scales by level difference; quest XP is fixed. Level-up raises health, resource and base damage.
- Loot tables per creature template: money plus weighted item drops and quest items (only while the quest is active). The corpse is lootable by its tapper (the first player to damage it).
- Inventory: 16 slots with stackable items. Equipment slots: main hand, off hand, head, chest, legs, feet. Gear adds stats (stamina → health, strength/agility/intellect → class damage). *Equipment core landed* (#66): item catalog revision 2 with seven starter pieces, humanoid drops in loot catalog revision 2 and content revision 7, `EquipItem`/`UnequipItem`, five health per stamina and half the class's primary stat as melee and direct-spell damage; the rules are in [INVENTORY.md](INVENTORY.md#equipment).
- A vendor NPC buys and sells. Money is copper, `u32`.

### Quests

- Objective kinds: kill N of template, collect N items (drops), talk to NPC, explore area, retrieve an item by swimming into an area (#274).
- The quest log holds at most 10 entries. Accept and turn-in happen at NPCs, and prerequisites form chains. Rewards: XP, money, one item choice.
- NPC markers are per-player: `!` available, `?` complete, grey `?` in progress.
- Chain: *Trouble in the Woods* (kill 6 wolves) → *Pelts for the Tanner* (collect 5 wolf pelts) → *The Missing Farmhand* (talk at Millbrook) → *Marauders in the Fields* (kill 8 marauders) → *Scout the Lake* (explore) → *Mirefin Menace* (kill 6 lurkers) → *Into Redbrand Hollow* (kill 10 bandits) → *Garrick Redbrand* (named boss) → *Return to Greyhaven*.

*Quests landed* (#25): `mmorpg_core::quest` holds the rules and `greyhaven_vale::quests` the chain (quest catalog 1, content revision 9). Each quest names a giver and an ender among the hub's quest givers (Marshal Elden Greywatch, Tanner Hilda Brook, Farmer Osric Mill), at most one prerequisite (an earlier quest), one to three objectives, XP, copper and up to four reward items to choose one from. `AcceptQuest`, `CompleteQuest` and `AbandonQuest` (command v9) resolve in tick step 1; tick step 8 completes talk and explore objectives; kills credit the corpse's tapper on the same terms as kill XP. The log, the turned-in mask and the markers travel in the self sheet (snapshot v14, see [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md#quests-snapshot-v14-and-command-v9)), and the canonical snapshot carries each log so recovery continues it exactly. - Accepting and turning in need a living player within 5 m (XZ) of the NPC's feet, like the vendor; abandoning works anywhere, also while dead, and loses the quest's progress.
- A talk objective completes when the player stands within that same 5 m of the NPC; an explore objective when the player stands inside the named area. Both are checked for living players after movement each tick.
- Collect progress is the bag's count of the item (capped at the required count), so selling, moving or a full bag change it, and the turn-in consumes exactly the required units. The quest item (Wolf Pelt, item catalog 3) drops as one unit on the corpse of the source template's creature when its tapper still needs more at the moment it dies, beside the ordinary loot and without drawing from the loot stream; it is claimed with the corpse. A quest item stays in the bag after an abandon.
- Markers rank `?` (a quest ending here is complete) over `!` (a quest starting here is available) over grey `?` (a quest ending here is active). They are computed from the log, the bag and the content for every quest giver and ender and sent with the sheet, not per entity record.
- Quest XP uses the same level-up path as kill XP; the reward choice is one unit of the chosen item and must be valid when choices exist (0 otherwise). A turn-in that cannot place the item or credit the copper changes nothing.
- Garrick Redbrand is the chain's boss as authored in revision 3. Since revision 22 (#276) he is a shared outdoor boss: his first engagement locks +60% health and +10% damage per extra participant already fighting the camp (up to five), every player who damages him or heals someone he fights earns the kill objective, he resets fully after an evade, his camp rallies at the mine mouth for 20 s when he falls, his corpse stays until he returns 2 min later, and he telegraphs Redbrand Whirl (a 1.5 s wind-up, then a 4 m ring that hurts and knocks back).
- Browser: Talk (T) opens the dialog of the selected or nearest projected quest NPC (quests offered, ready with the reward choice, or in progress), Quests (L) the log with Abandon, the HUD tracks every active objective, and `!`/`?` markers float over NPCs on screen. Progress the zone confirms between two received sheets briefly highlights its tracker line, a small non-blocking notice reports a completed objective or quest, and the ender's `?` pulses when it becomes ready (#164); the first sheet after a reset or reconnect only synchronizes, so nothing is announced twice. The native client decodes the quest sheet but does not present it yet.

### Player-scoped projection

- Header, then a **self** section: exact health/resource/xp/level/target/cast/GCD/cooldowns/auras.
- **Entities**: nearest relevant units within the interest radius (45 m), capped by count in `(distance, kind, id)` priority, and always including the player's current target. Relevance keeps at most 64 units; records are 21 bytes, and budget-driven packing writes as many as fit one datagram (a 1,077-byte budget: 47 records without events/sheet, 44 with a bag, 33 beside a bag and full event section, 32 beside bag/loot and full events); see [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md#datagram-byte-budget). `game-server` now fragments oversized session frames, while a future MMO wire change must revise the projection budget for additional sections. Each record carries kind, id, template/appearance, position, velocity, facing, level, health percent and flags (dead, in combat, hostile, attackable, tapped by other, evading, targets the viewer, lootable by the viewer); later steps add quest-marker and casting flags and cast progress. Instead of a per-entity target, a target section carries the viewer's target-of-target.
- **Events**: bounded feedback such as damage/heal/miss, XP, loot, quest updates, level-up and errors ("Out of range", "Not enough mana"). They are cosmetic and may be lost.
- **Sheet**: money, inventory, equipment and quest log. It is included when changed and periodically every 10 ticks, so loss self-heals within about 330 ms.
- **Names**: player display names are sent in a periodic section. Creature and NPC names come from content by template ID.

### Presentation

Presentation never feeds back into gameplay; it reads projections and the Rust scenery export and sends intent.

- **Scenery export** (`mmorpg-wasm`, format `mmorpg.scenery`): the terrain grid (4 m, ±280 m), a far ring of the same relief (20 m, ±600 m), a prop kind table with compact per-prop records (kind, feet anchor on the relief, yaw, scale, body box), every structure's exact collider box, roads, water, areas and an independent presentation fingerprint for resource keys. The saved Outpost grass mask is presentation-only. `mmorpg-scenery` stays presentation-only; hosts never link it.
- **Browser world** (step 12a): vertex-coloured terrain with soft biome transitions and grass tones; roads, plaza, field and shore as smooth surfaces; fog-softened far ranges above a 50 m snow line; procedural low-poly models for every prop kind, repeated vegetation and rocks sharing immutable instance geometry and other parts merged per chunk, colour and cull class, with walls at body height on their colliders; a translucent lake; a renderer sky with a matching CSS backdrop; animated humanoids with class gear through a unit model registry keyed by entity kind, whose models read the whole projected entity and place feet by their own body height; a camera that stays above the ground, eases its zoom and turns the character on a right drag; a circular minimap; and an F3 overlay with node counts and renderer work.
- **Renderer adoption** (#172): the current pinned 3d-lab renderer consumes vertex colours, instance batches, fog, sky/ambient and directional light. A cosmetic twenty-minute noon/dusk/night/dawn cycle drives the actual frame environment, with moonlit ground and readable targets at night; reduced motion holds noon. Lamps, lit windows, waystone runes and the staff orb are emissive; target rings are unlit. Tree and rock variants and a bounded family of grass tufts share geometry, while affine instance matrices retain authored placement and dimensions. Instanced chunks use the same distance classes and exact transformed bounds; picking intersects the actual submitted instance transforms. Static draw-group statistics count both ordinary and instance batches; vertex/triangle statistics count unique geometry storage. This is presentation-only and never drives gameplay or NPC schedules.
- **Procedural action playback** (#27): ordinary attacks consume explicit server swing occurrences, accepted abilities animate the specific player or creature, and existing Muck Bolt/Mend casts hold and release through their real lifecycle. Generic/periodic/channel damage does not invent an attack. Positive health damage adds bounded target-local impact sparks; observers receive neutral facts through existing visibility, never another player's damage credit. Player and creature death overrides actions, plays only on an observed live-to-dead transition and settles without replay on hydration or interest reentry. Ranger keeps the existing rigid packaged mesh, using its supported whole-body action transform rather than claiming an authored arm rig. NPC idle stays independent; this adds no NPC combat or schedules. Reduced motion, interest exit, world exit and reconnect retire transient presentation state.
- **Creature and NPC models** (step 12b, browser, now `crates/mmorpg-presentation/src/units/creatures.rs`, shared by both clients (#184)): the registry's creature and NPC entries pick a procedural low-poly body by catalog family or NPC role. Wolves, boars and rats are quadrupeds with a diagonal-pair trot (`quadrupedPoseFor`: phase from distance travelled, reach from horizontal speed, idle breathing at rest) that roll onto their side when dead; Field Marauders, Mirefin Lurkers, Redbrand Bandits, Garrick (the elite template, larger through its box and a darker cloak) and the guard, vendor, quest giver and spirit healer are compact humanoids posed by `poseFor` that lie flat when dead. Each model fits its template's collision box (NPCs the 0.6 m × 1.8 m character box) within 10% and draws at most 24 nodes with its target ring. The disposition colour stays an accent (animal eyes and collar, humanoid clothing) and corpses keep the shade; unknown templates fall back to the placeholder box.
- **Later presentation**: selection circles beyond the target ring and nameplates, and native client parity (step 13). Simple spell visuals on existing renderer nodes landed with step 8b.

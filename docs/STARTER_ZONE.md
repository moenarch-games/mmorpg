# Greyhaven Vale starter zone


Current landscape work (#644) moves Stillwater Lake and its shrine into the western forest,
with a short outlet to the western waterfall and the existing village brook. The original
terrain-pass history below describes the former eastern placement; the current target and
placement details are in [Greyhaven landscape work](GREYHAVEN_BROOK.md#revised-landscape-target-owner-brief-2026-11-06).

Target: a playable tech demo comparable in *shape* to a classic MMO starter area. A new level-1 character leaves the Greyhaven Outpost hub, fights creatures in surrounding subzones, completes a short quest chain, gains levels and gear, and meets a named boss. Every gameplay outcome stays server-authoritative, deterministic and replay/recovery-complete.

This document is the design contract for that slice. The implementation plan below is tracked as GitHub issues. Each step lands as its own pull request.

The browser demo runs the same `ZoneSimulation` through a WASM build as a local, single-player zone host ([ADR 0002](adr/0002-browser-embeds-zone-simulation.md)). The browser presentation consumes the same player-scoped wire projections that the network host sends. It never reads canonical state.

## Invariants every step keeps

- One writer per zone epoch; fencing, handoff and interest contracts are unchanged.
- Gameplay failures are **outcomes**, not transport errors. A command that is well-formed but not currently allowed, such as an ability on cooldown or an out-of-range target, is accepted by `apply_command`, produces a player-visible feedback event, and never closes the session. Only malformed payloads return errors.
- Discrete intents such as casting, looting or accepting quests are processed during the tick in `(player_id, sequence)` order. They are never applied inside `apply_command` against a partially advanced world.
- All authoritative state enters the canonical snapshot: units, auras, cooldowns, AI state, spawn timers, corpses, loot, inventories, quest progress and the zone and per-creature RNG states. Recovery at any tick must reproduce the continuation exactly (tested per step).
- Iteration over units is ordered (`BTreeMap` or sorted `Vec`). No `HashMap` iteration, wall clock or floating point decides authoritative state. Integer trigonometry uses a const-generated table.
- Snapshot projections are bounded: a deterministic priority cap on entity count plus a byte budget test. Events per snapshot are bounded, and losing a datagram may drop cosmetic events only. Durable facts such as quest progress, inventory and XP also live in periodically re-sent state.
- Content (collision, creature templates, items, quests, abilities) is identified by the zone content revision. Changing it requires a new revision.

## Coordinates and scale

- 100 simulation units per metre, Y up, 60 Hz ticks.
- The playable square is about 416 m × 416 m, centred on the origin (#159: the original 240 m valley plus a ring of four outer regions — Greyfang Ridge, Southmeadow Downs, Hollowpine Reach and Eastwater Fen — joined by the Vale Ring Road, the Fen Track and the Downs Trail). Mountains outside are scenery.
- **The whole Greyhaven Vale region** (#810, Atlas 5.1.i810, `greyhaven_vale::region`): the zone covers the continent atlas's Greyhaven Vale region, about 3.7 km × 2.6 km (4.9 km²), round the hand-authored Vale, Farmland, Highlands and Wetlands (`greyhaven_vale::AUTHORED`). Its outline and base terrain (40 m atlas samples, −45 m to +254 m relative to the Vale's floor) are exported once by `atlas.py zone-export greyhaven` into `content/greyhaven/region.json`, which is zone content from then on. The 4 m terrain grid covers the region in whole physics tiles; the authored ground is unchanged and blends into the base terrain over 60 m beyond it. Invisible boundary walls (20 m above and below the ground, solid from both sides) follow the outline; beyond them the region's mountains and the distant ranges are scenery. One host simulates the whole region; combat does not cross zone borders.
- **The region as one landscape** (#816, Atlas 5.1.i816 and 5.1.i816-2, [design](GREYHAVEN_REGION.md)): beyond the authored heart round the Outpost's basin the ground is designed terrain from `content/greyhaven/design.json`, with the Greywater, Fenwater, Delving Run and Westmere Tarn, a graded road network with fords, organic forests, rock fields, seven named sub-regions and their creatures. The outer valley's square ring and its Vale Ring Road, the escarpment and its Highland Pass (now a winding saddle road over Greyfang Ridge), the farmland's ridge and notch, the wetlands' ridge and pass and their climbing routes are gone; the paragraphs below describe the regions as first built.

The outer valley (#159, content revision 13) has physical hills up to 10 m: an authored 4 m heightfield (`greyhaven_vale::terrain()`) that the zone's physics world walks units on, with flat cells left to the floor half-space. Clients draw the terrain as physical ground plus the presentation relief (at most about 0.6 m) and draw units at their physics position plus that relief.

- **Dodge roll** (#516, Atlas 5.1.i516, `mmorpg_core::roll`): tap F (pad B; Shift until #294) to roll about 3.5 m in 0.6 s along the held movement, or straight back standing still, with a 1 s cooldown after it. The three Greyfang outriders are Redbrand Crossbowmen (levels 3–5) whose Crossbow Bolt (1 s wind-up, 8 s cooldown, 20 m, 5–8 damage) is a tracking bolt. Both clients draw placeholder shafts until asset-tooling's bow/arrow bundle lands.

Pulled, the pair arrive first: Frost Nova (8 m) roots them while the lookout is still outside it; backing off draws the lookout away from them, so Chain Lightning on a rooted bandit jumps to the other (8 m, in line of sight) and not to the lookout, and its damage breaks both roots. Both clients hold an ice ring at each Nova-rooted unit's feet from the zone's `AuraApplied` until its `AuraRemoved` (expiry or a breaking hit).
- **Rimewood signal post** (#952, `greyhaven_vale::signal_post`, spawn IDs 2800–2819): a target-priority fight 50 m west of the scouts, at (−620 m, −330 m). - **Tinker reference encounter** (#950, Atlas 5.2.i950-2; specification, the Tinker class is not implemented): the one three-enemy fight that tests the Tinker's **prepare → attract → combine → detonate** against throwing bombs on cooldown. It reuses the Rimewood scouts (#933, spawns 1800–1802, no new spawns): the side-by-side pair stand 1.5 m apart at `rimewood::PAIR_AT` (−570 m, −330 m), the lookout 6 m west and 4 m south of them (about 7 m from either), all level-5 Redbrand bandits facing east on level ground. The Tinker comes from the east, the approach the pair face. Inclusion is geometry only, through existing rules: a flask splashes hostile units within 3 m of where it shatters that the point can see (Venom Flask's `Splash`, #290); a bomb bursts on the unit it touches or its fuse and blasts units within 4 m with line of sight from the burst, falling off to the edge (Blasting Bomb's `Blast`, like Fire Blast); a world trap acts only on units whose body overlaps its volume (`mmorpg_core::trap`, #300, Atlas 5.2.i300-4). A unit outside all three volumes is never hit, so the lookout, 7 m off, is excluded unless it walks in. Moving units dodge: a bomb lobbed 14 m flies about 1.5 s, and a pair running at the thrower pass under it, which is why holding them matters.
  - **Stage 1** (flask → bomb, no trap): a Tinker 14.5 m east of the pair lobs a volatile flask on them (both inside its 3 m, the lookout not), lets them charge into reach, then bombs them standing there; the bomb's burst on flasked units adds the chemical/explosive payoff to exactly those inside the blast and nothing outside it. `crates/mmorpg-core/tests/combat/tinker_setup.rs` already stages this geometry with the Rogue's Venom Flask and Blasting Bomb (no payoff yet) and asserts the pair are flasked and blasted while the lookout is untouched, replaying exactly. The prototype adds the payoff and reports whether flask-then-bomb beats two bombs on cooldown.
  - **Stage 2** (the full sequence): before the pull, the Tinker places one visible mechanical trap on the pair's approach line, about 6 m east of `PAIR_AT`, reusing trap validation, the `TriggerVolume` overlap test, canonical firing and per-unit hit memory (#300, #302's player-placed traps). Pulled, the pair run onto it and are held where they stand; the flask and then the bomb land on two units that cannot step out from under them, while the lookout, trailing about 7 m behind, stays outside the trap, the 3 m splash and the 4 m blast. Spring it early, throw at a running pair, or let the lookout catch up and the setup is lost.
  - Open for the prototype (not decided here): trap count, lifetime, hold effect and its duration, the payoff's damage, cooldowns, and which throwables and traps the Tinker shares with the Rogue (5.2.73–5.2.74 stand; no Rogue ability or asset moves).

**World traps** (#300, Atlas 5.2.i300-4, `mmorpg_core::trap`, `greyhaven_vale::traps`): pressure-plate falling rocks in the Greyfang cavern's mouth (plate at x 59.5, z −141.5 m, off the walk from the mouth to the passage), two swinging blades in the 4 m passage to the inner chamber, and a spike pit in the Redbrand camp (x −18, z −86 m), all with visible tells and none hidden. Creatures **navigate around blockers** (#307): a creature chasing a target or running home whose capsule, swept straight above knee height, would meet a wall, a structure piece, rubble or a closed door walks an A* route on a 1 m grid instead (cells tested by physics overlap queries, cached until a door moves or a piece breaks or regrows), steering at the farthest of the next six route cells it can reach straight. So a creature boxed in by pieces comes out once a wall breaks, and goes around a broken post's rubble. Spell and arrow sight lines follow the pieces that stand, since a broken piece's collider leaves the world.

## Systems

### Movement

- Command `Move { forward: -1..=1, strafe: -1..=1, facing: u16 }`. The server rotates local intent by `facing` through the integer trig table. Run speed is 21 units/tick (6.3 m/s); a backward component uses 13 units/tick. Facing is intent and has no collision effect.
- Command `Jump`: takes effect only when grounded. A thin overlap query just below the feet decides that; touching any other body (geometry or another unit) counts, side contact with a wall does not. Vertical velocity is set to a constant, and physics integrates gravity. The next tick consumes the intent either way, so an airborne jump is ignored rather than buffered.
- Moving interrupts casts. Dead units cannot move.

### Units and combat

- A unit is a player, creature or NPC. `EntityRef = Player(u32) | Creature(u32) | Npc(u32)` on the wire as `kind: u8, id: u32`. Physics body IDs occupy disjoint namespaces per kind.
- Unit state: level, health/max, resource kind/value/max, faction, target, cast state, auras (bounded), combat flag, death state.
- Players: `SelectTarget(EntityRef | none)`, `Swing { facing }`, `UseAbility { ability, target }`. The server validates range, aim and resource; offensive abilities fire where the player faces and the target names only a friendly ability's recipient (#517).
- A swing (#517) is a movement-state strike (#885): a standing three-hit chain, a running lunge or a jumping landing blow, timed and shaped by the weapon family. It winds up and hits everything in its arc and reach where the player faces on its contact tick, targeted or not; one that hits nothing whiffs. Damage is `base + level scaling`, scaled per strike, with deterministic variance from the zone RNG. Crits apply ×1.5.
- Wolves, boars, rats, bears, cave spiders and marsh lurkers wind up each attack (#889): the quick bite two times in three, the slower heavy one dealing half again, landing on the clip's contact frame, so a player who steps away or raises a shield in time avoids it.
- The Forest Lynx (#989) pounces instead: it engages a player within 4 m, stalks in at a walk to 1.37 m beyond its reach, crouches still through its 0.5 s wind-up while turning toward its target, leaps the gap to its reach along its facing on the lunge's take-off and lands on the lunge's contact (0.833 s after the start), then holds still for its 0.833 s recovery. Stepping aside during the wind-up dodges it; the recovery is the opening to hit back. Three lie up on Southwood's eastern and southern edge (spawns 3100–3102), off the Southwood Trail.
- The Marsh Adder (#990) warns instead: it engages a player within 4 m, slithers after them at 1.2 m/s (slower than a walk), and once in reach coils still through one 1.333 s `Warn_Hiss_Loop` while turning toward its target, then strikes on `Strike`'s contact (1.541 s after the start) and holds still for the rest of the strike and its 0.833 s `Strike_Recover` (1.042 s). Backing off during the hiss dodges it; the recovery is the opening to hit back. It has no poison. Three lie coiled in Adder Hollow, Eastwater Fen's low western edge 70 m and more south of the Fen Road (spawns 3120–3122), and drop a little coin.
- The Cave Bat (#995) flies (`mmorpg_core::flight`): its body is weightless and the zone sets only its height, so the physics step keeps it off walls, roof and floor and it never hits through rock. It hangs still at its roost, its centre 3.2 m over the ground, until a player comes within 4 m; then it drops off, flies after them at 4.2 m/s (a running player outpaces it) with its centre 1.7 m over theirs, above a sword's height band, and dives from 2 m beyond its reach: it hovers through `Dive_Windup`'s 0.5 s flaring hiss turning toward its target, stoops 2 m across and 1.4 m down along its facing to `Dive_Contact`'s contact (0.833 s after the start), bites only a target within reach and a step and within 1 m of its height, and hangs low and still through the rest of the dive and `Dive_Recover` (1 s), where a sword reaches it, before it climbs back. Stepping away through the wind-up dodges it. It gives a fight up 15 m from its roost, so the cave mouth is always an escape, and its corpse lies on the ground where it fell. Two roost on the outer cavern's stalactite by the north mouth's passage (spawns 3140–3141) and drop a little coin.
- The River Crayfish (#996) is a positional opponent: its front shell and claws turn a melee blow, arrow or bolt from inside its 60° front half-arc exactly as the Redbrand Brute's shield does (#953; no damage, the swinger sees Blocked), and it turns toward its target only at the engaged 120°/s, so a blow from its side or rear lands. It engages a player within 4 m, scuttles after them at 1.5 m/s (slower than a walk) and from its reach holds still through `Attack_A`'s claw raise, turning toward its target, pinches on the clip's contact (0.75 s after the start) and holds the committed pinch through the clip's rest (0.5 s). Spells and stuns get past the shell. Three lie on the Fenwater's north bank in the alders some 100 m upstream of Fenford's ford, on the low margin 1.2 m beyond the channel's edge (spawns 3160–3162); none swims. They drop a little coin.
- The Southwood Stag (#997) is neutral but territorial: it grazes, a player may watch it from beyond 7 m, and it fights anyone who attacks it or comes within 7 m of it while idle. It walks to 2 m beyond its reach, stands head up and still through its 1 s `Alert` turning toward its target, then charges 2 m along its facing over `Attack_A`'s root-travel window (0.333–0.625 s into the clip), lands on its contact (1.625 s after the start) through the ordinary hit table, and stands blown through the rest of `Attack_A` (0.708 s). Stepping aside as it charges dodges it; the recovery is the opening to hit back. The release's rearing `Attack_B` is not used. Two graze the open meadow on Southwood's western edge (spawns 3180–3181), 28 m and more off any road and far from the wood's other creatures, and drop a hide three times in four and a little coin. The ambient stags (#857) are unchanged scenery.
- Death: creatures leave a lootable corpse. Players drop to a dead state and `ReleaseSpirit` respawns them at the graveyard with half health. Health and resource regenerate out of combat.

Step 8a implemented the kit in core (abilities 1–12 of the catalog; lurkers cast Muck Bolt, 13, and bandits Crude Bandage, 14, in content revision 6). Its numbers are in `mmorpg_core::ABILITY_CATALOG` and the rules in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md#units-combat-and-creature-ai). Recorded decisions:

- Cleave hits the target and up to two more living creatures within 3 m of the target in `EntityRef` order, for weapon damage each, instead of a frontal arc.
- "Per level" amounts scale with the caster's levels above 1, like the player damage curve; weapon damage is one draw from the player's melee range. Ability damage has no miss or critical roll.
- Rage comes from swing hits dealt and from every creature hit taken (swing or ability), even a fully absorbed one; death drains it.
- Durations, cooldowns, the global cooldown and the five-second rule count the tick they start in; damage and heal over time pulse every period from there.
- A cast completes against its target without a second range check; a target that died ends it as interrupted. Targeted ranged abilities need line of sight at use and again at completion: a deterministic physics ray between body centres must not hit static content colliders (units never block), otherwise the new `LineOfSight` error refuses it at no cost. Static content means every body in the static collider namespace: content boxes, door leaves, ramps (#59) and the mill hoist (#56); a 2-unit plank blocks, a gap lets the line through, and equally near hits are broken by body ID on both physics backends (#57). The browser's WASM build reaches the same sight decisions and projections as the native build (`web/tests/sight-parity.test.ts` runs the native sessions of `crates/mmorpg-wasm/tests/sight_parity.rs` and compares their digests live with the WASM digests). Since #1109 authoritative float code uses `libm` rather than the platform's C library, and the digest covers the whole projection, broken props' debris included. Since #1150 the scripted physical sessions of `crates/mmorpg-wasm/tests/parity-sessions.json` also compare a slope walk, the boulder trap, a Frost Breath cone, melee fights with a bandit and a wolf and two characters walking into each other, digesting the canonical snapshot as well as the projections. A Warden's **Charge** also needs a path as wide as its body (#57, Atlas 5.2.79): the Warden's capsule, raised to knee height (32 units, so ground, slopes, kerbs and the campfire do not count), swept toward the target until melee reach must meet no static geometry, or the charge is refused with `LineOfSight` at no cost; a sight line threading a gap narrower than the Warden is not enough. A creature's Muck Bolt neither starts nor lands without line of sight (a broken line fizzles as interrupted). A creature's cast at a player who dies or leaves ends silently.
- Invalid or repeated class choices report the new `InvalidClass` error.
- Heroic Strike also has a **directed mode** (#154, Atlas 5.2.27/5.2.53/5.2.55): the same ability, unlock, Rage cost, cooldown and global cooldown, executed by a `DirectedStrike` command with a facing and a left/centre/right, low/middle/high aim. Core resolves the sweep's physical coverage (a ±30° arc turned 25° toward the side, so it also covers a unit straight ahead (#884), melee reach, the physics body's overlap with the height band, static-geometry line of sight) and strikes the nearest covered creature for the weapon-strike damage; a sweep covering nobody whiffs but still pays. A high slash passes over a wolf; a low one reaches a rat. See [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md#commands).
- A Warden can hold a **directional shield guard** (#156, Atlas 5.2.29–5.2.68) with a `Guard` command naming the low, middle or high lane. The guard protects only while the player is a living, unstunned Warden standing on ground (the jump probe; a jump or fall suspends it, Atlas 5.2.63) and not recovering from a break. A creature melee hit that rolled damage meets a protecting guard when its lane matches and it comes from the shield's coverage (Atlas 5.2.68): the lane is the height band containing the attacker's contact point, three quarters up its physics body, above the defender's feet (rats low, wolves and boars middle, humanoids high), and the bearing from the defender to the attacker must lie within ±75° of the facing turned 30° toward the shield (left) side, so the front and shield flank are covered and the sword flank and rear are not. Its impact is the rolled damage plus the attacker's physics velocity toward the defender (units per tick) times its body volume over the defender's. Below 18 the blow is fully blocked (no damage, no Rage); at 18 or more it overpowers: the guard breaks for 45 ticks (1.5 s) and the blow lands in full (Atlas 5.2.33/5.2.35). Stationary blows of ordinary creatures are blocked; a charging wolf, boar or humanoid and the heaviest critical hits overpower. The hold survives a break, so the same lane protects again when recovery ends (Atlas 5.2.59). A protecting guard slows horizontal movement to 60 % (Atlas 5.2.61); a suspended or broken one does not. Guarding costs nothing (Atlas 5.2.66). Death and a directed strike (the last-pressed gesture, Atlas 5.2.37) lower the guard; only melee swings are blocked, never abilities. A fully blocked blow grants no Rage, unlike an absorbed one. See [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md#commands).
- Class-specific health and damage, Ranger Auto Shot, Garrick's abilities, the action bar and cast bars are out of this step (8b and later).

### Creature AI

- States: `Idle` (wander within a radius using its own random stream, #861), `Engaged` (chase to melee or cast range, attack on the swing timer), `Evading` (leash beyond 40 m from spawn or no valid threat: run home, reset to full health, ignore damage), `Dead` (corpse until timeout, then a respawn timer; claiming rewards does not shorten this schedule).
- Aggro radius is 10 m ± 1 m per level difference, clamped. Nearby same-family creatures assist when one is engaged.
- Idle land creatures with no player within 60 m sleep: no AI and no physics step. Awake idle ones decide on one tick in ten, staggered by creature, and walk on in between, so they notice a player up to a sixth of a second late. Fighting, evading, trailing and rallying creatures, swimmers and raiders decide every tick (#814).
- The threat table is ordered by `(threat desc, entity asc)`.
- Steering is direct toward the target. Physics blocks obstacles; no navmesh in this slice.

### Progression, loot and economy

- Experience curve for levels 1–10. Kill XP scales by level difference; quest XP is fixed. Level-up raises health, resource and base damage.

An evidence spot is zone content (`content/greyhaven/quests.json`'s `evidence`: a stable ID, a name, a spot and a reach of 2–2.5 m) with its art in the world dressing (the cart scene's props, and `dressing/evidence.json` for the new scraps, rag, crates and strongbox, presentation only like the cart scene). Interact (E) at a spot an active quest still needs sends `Inspect`; native and browser pick the nearest such spot through `mmorpg-client-model`. The zone checks it in tick step 1: a living player with the weapon sheathed, outside a handoff corridor, feet within the spot's reach and 2 m of its height, and an active quest whose inspect objective of that spot is still open. Then every such objective is done at once and stored in the log, so snapshots, replay and saved characters carry it. An unknown spot is refused as an invalid target, one out of reach as out of range, and one no active quest needs (before accepting, or already read) as an invalid quest; refusals change nothing, so the player simply tries again. **Tactical and social quests (#1019, slice 2).** Seven more Core Vale quests (35, 37–42) bring the catalogue to 42. Their rules are core; both clients show them through the shared client model and the zone checks everything again.

- *Quest actions at NPCs.* With an NPC selected, Interact sends `QuestAction` when an active quest acts on it (`mmorpg-client-model`'s `quest_action_at`, otherwise it talks). The zone performs, in tick step 1, the first open use-item, escort or interview objective at that NPC, in log order, whose quest's earlier objectives are done: a living player with the weapon sheathed, outside a handoff corridor and within quest reach of a visible NPC. Nothing open is an invalid quest; refusals change nothing.
- *Supplies and loans.* A quest's `supplies` are handed over on accepting (one of each meant for the class, unless already held; a full bag refuses the accept) and taken back on abandoning. A plain bag item left at the turn-in goes back; equipment is a loan the turn-in requires, from the bag or the hands. Vendors refuse supplies and loans alike (`NotSellable`), so a quest retaken after a sale never mints copper.
- *Use item* consumes one of the item from the bag. A `confrontations` NPC shown its evidence turns hostile: its reserved foe stands a metre away and attacks the presenter, the NPC is gone (no projection, body or interaction) while the foe stands or lies dead, and a foe out of its fight leaves the world so the NPC is back.
- *Interview* asks an NPC with an `attitudes` entry: it refuses a distrusted class (`Distrusted`, with the actionable text) unless the player turned in its recommendation quest or a party mate of a class it trusts stands within quest reach of it. Sponsoring completes nothing for the sponsor. No main-chain quest is gated.
- *Escort* starts an escort: the NPC follows the player's trail (points 1 m apart, at most 64) at 1.8 m/s, stopping 2 m behind. Inside the objective's area it arrives: the objective is done for the escorting player and for party mates with it open within quest reach, and the NPC rests there 1 min before it is back at its post. A player more than 30 m ahead for 10 s, a dead or absent player or an abandoned quest fails it (`EscortLost`): the NPC is back at once and the player asks again. One player escorts an NPC at a time (`NpcBusy`). Escorts are canonical zone state.
- *Defeat with a loan* counts tapper kills of its template while the quest's loan is in the main hand, with kill credit's receipts.
- *Crush* counts each impact of a boulder the player set rolling on a living creature of its template (one roll hits a victim once); a creature's death to anything else counts nothing.
- *Demolish* completes when the player's own blast or shot destroys the named structure piece.

Items 36–41 are the three trial arms and the bandage, draught and toll pass; template 27 is the Redbrand Courier (spawn 2930, reserved). The world map's approximate quest areas (#983) cover inspect objectives by the area their spot lies in.

**Carried loads (#1077).** Players carry loads (owner decision 2026-11-23) for delivery objectives, and *The Last Sack* (43, #1019's slot) is the first: Hilda sends the player back to the Wolfrun cart for the last sack of Osric's missing load. The rules live in `mmorpg-core`'s `carry` module, and both clients act through the shared client model while the zone checks everything again.

- *Sources.* A load source is zone content (`content/greyhaven/quests.json`'s `loads`: a stable ID, a name, a kind and a spot with a reach of at most 5 m). The kinds are humanoid-equipment's `load.*` items: crate, sack, firewood, hay, basket and water jug. A source hands a load only to a player whose active quest still has a delivery of it open, as often as asked, so a lost load is fetched again and the quest goes on.
- *Lift and set down.* Interact (E) next to the player's own set-down load lifts it, and next to an open delivery's source sends `Lift` (`lift_in_reach`). With a load in both arms and no NPC selected, Interact sends `SetDown`. The zone checks both in tick step 1. The player must be living, with feet on the ground, out of deep water, off any face and not rolling (`NoFooting`), and within 2 m (and 2 m of height) of the source or the load. Only one load at a time (`HandsFull`). Lifting sheathes the weapon and stops a sit, a repair and a cast. A set-down load stays where it was put for 5 min, visible to everyone nearby, and only its carrier lifts it again. A fresh load from the source replaces it.
- *While carrying.* Swings, weapon draws, guards, every ability (authored ones too), sitting, repairs, gathering, skinning and waystones are refused with `HandsFull` and its actionable text. So are a dodge roll and a jump onto a climbable face or ledge: the carrier grabs nothing. No speed boost carries a carrier past the normal run speed; Greyhaven has no sprint. Clients see the load in the player's entity flags and play `Carry_Walk_Loop`, or `Heavy_Run_Fwd_Loop` under `Carry_Run_Loop` at run speed, with the item on `chest.carry`.
- *Delivery.* A `deliver_load` objective names a source and an NPC. With that NPC selected and the load in both arms, Interact sends `QuestAction` (the slice-2 path), and the zone takes the load and completes the objective. Empty arms are refused with `NotCarrying`.
- *Losing it.* A carrier who dies, logs out or hands off sets the load down where they stand when the footing allows; otherwise it is gone. A carrier in deep water loses it (`LoadLost`). Abandoning the quest drops a load no other active quest delivers. A load is never an item: no bag, vendor, bank or saved character holds it, so nothing can be sold or duplicated. Loads are canonical zone state, and the carry step visits each one once a tick (`carry-step` in `.performance/contract.json`).

The first quest keeps its stable ID, so characters who turned in *Trouble in the Woods* still have it done, and the downstream prerequisites are unchanged. A saved log is fitted on admission (`quest::fit_log`): progress stored past a revised objective's count counts as that objective done. A character saved mid-hunt (up to 6 wolves) therefore loads with the woods explored and the sacks still to fetch.

- Accepting and turning in need a living player within 5 m (XZ) of the NPC's feet, like the vendor; abandoning works anywhere, also while dead, and loses the quest's progress.
- A talk objective completes when the player stands within that same 5 m of the NPC; an explore objective when the player stands inside the named area. Both are checked for living players after movement each tick.
- Collect progress is the bag's count of the item (capped at the required count), so selling, moving or a full bag change it, and the turn-in consumes exactly the required units. The quest item (Wolf Pelt, item catalog 3) drops as one unit on the corpse of the source template's creature when its tapper still needs more at the moment it dies, beside the ordinary loot and without drawing from the loot stream; it is claimed with the corpse. A quest item stays in the bag after an abandon.
- Markers rank `?` (a quest ending here is complete) over `!` (a quest starting here is available) over grey `?` (a quest ending here is active). They are computed from the log, the bag and the content for every quest giver and ender and sent with the sheet, not per entity record.
- Quest XP uses the same level-up path as kill XP; the reward choice is one unit of the chosen item and must be valid when choices exist (0 otherwise). A turn-in that cannot place the item or credit the copper changes nothing.
- Garrick Redbrand is the chain's boss as authored in revision 3. Since revision 22 (#276) he is a shared outdoor boss: his first engagement locks +60% health and +10% damage per extra participant already fighting the camp (up to five), every player who damages him or heals someone he fights earns the kill objective, he resets fully after an evade, his camp rallies at the mine mouth for 20 s when he falls, his corpse stays until he returns 2 min later, and he telegraphs Redbrand Whirl (a 1.5 s wind-up, then a 4 m ring that hurts and knocks back).
- Browser: Talk (E) opens the dialog of the selected or nearest projected quest NPC (quests offered, ready with the reward choice, or in progress), Quests (L) the log with Abandon, the HUD tracks every active objective, and `!`/`?` markers float over NPCs on screen. Progress the zone confirms between two received sheets briefly highlights its tracker line, a small non-blocking notice reports a completed objective or quest, and the ender's `?` pulses when it becomes ready (#164); the first sheet after a reset or reconnect only synchronizes, so nothing is announced twice. The native client decodes the quest sheet but does not present it yet.

### Player-scoped projection

- Header, then a **self** section: exact health/resource/xp/level/target/cast/GCD/cooldowns/auras.
- **Entities**: nearest relevant units within the interest radius (45 m), capped by count in `(distance, kind, id)` priority, and always including the player's current target. Relevance keeps at most 64 units; records are 21 bytes, and budget-driven packing writes as many as fit one datagram (a 1,077-byte budget: 47 records without events/sheet, 44 with a bag, 33 beside a bag and full event section, 32 beside bag/loot and full events); see [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md#datagram-byte-budget). `game-server` now fragments oversized session frames, while a future MMO wire change must revise the projection budget for additional sections. Each record carries kind, id, template/appearance, position, velocity, facing, level, health percent and flags (dead, in combat, hostile, attackable, tapped by other, evading, targets the viewer, lootable by the viewer); later steps add quest-marker and casting flags and cast progress. Instead of a per-entity target, a target section carries the viewer's target-of-target.
- **Events**: bounded feedback such as damage/heal/miss, XP, loot, quest updates, level-up and errors ("Out of range", "Not enough mana"). They are cosmetic and may be lost.
- **Sheet**: money, inventory, equipment and quest log. It is included when changed and periodically every 10 ticks, so loss self-heals within about 330 ms.
- **Names**: player display names are sent in a periodic section. Creature and NPC names come from content by template ID.

### Presentation

Presentation never feeds back into gameplay; it reads projections and the Rust scenery export and sends intent.

- **Scenery export** (`mmorpg-wasm`, format `mmorpg.scenery`): the terrain grid (4 m, ±280 m), a far ring of the same relief (20 m, ±600 m), a prop kind table with compact per-prop records (kind, feet anchor on the relief, yaw, scale, body box), every structure's exact collider box, roads, water, areas and an independent presentation fingerprint for resource keys. The saved Outpost grass mask is presentation-only. `mmorpg-scenery` stays presentation-only; hosts never link it.
- **Browser world** (step 12a): vertex-coloured terrain with soft biome transitions and grass tones; roads, plaza, field and shore as smooth surfaces; fog-softened far ranges above a 50 m snow line; procedural low-poly models for every prop kind, repeated vegetation and rocks sharing immutable instance geometry and other parts merged per chunk, colour and cull class, with walls at body height on their colliders; a translucent lake; a renderer sky with a matching CSS backdrop; animated humanoids with class gear through a unit model registry keyed by entity kind, whose models read the whole projected entity and place feet by their own body height; a camera that stays above the ground, eases its zoom and turns the character on a right drag; a circular minimap; and an F3 overlay with node counts and renderer work.
- **Renderer adoption** (#172): the current pinned 3d-lab renderer consumes vertex colours, instance batches, fog, sky/ambient and directional light. A cosmetic twenty-minute noon/dusk/night/dawn cycle drives the actual frame environment, with moonlit ground and readable targets at night; reduced motion holds noon. Lamps, lit windows, waystone runes and the staff orb are emissive; target rings are unlit. Tree and rock variants and a bounded family of grass tufts share geometry, while affine instance matrices retain authored placement and dimensions. Instanced chunks use the same distance classes and exact transformed bounds; picking intersects the actual submitted instance transforms. Static draw-group statistics count both ordinary and instance batches; vertex/triangle statistics count unique geometry storage. This is presentation-only and never drives gameplay or NPC schedules.
- **Procedural action playback** (#27): ordinary attacks consume explicit server swing occurrences, accepted abilities animate the specific player or creature, and existing Muck Bolt/Mend casts hold and release through their real lifecycle. Generic/periodic/channel damage does not invent an attack. Positive health damage adds bounded target-local impact sparks; observers receive neutral facts through existing visibility, never another player's damage credit. Player and creature death overrides actions, plays only on an observed live-to-dead transition and settles without replay on hydration or interest reentry. Ranger keeps the existing rigid packaged mesh, using its supported whole-body action transform rather than claiming an authored arm rig. NPC idle stays independent; this adds no NPC combat or schedules. Reduced motion, interest exit, world exit and reconnect retire transient presentation state.
- **Creature and NPC models** (step 12b, browser, now `crates/mmorpg-presentation/src/units/creatures.rs`, shared by both clients (#184)): the registry's creature and NPC entries pick a procedural low-poly body by catalog family or NPC role. Wolves, boars and rats are quadrupeds with a diagonal-pair trot (`quadrupedPoseFor`: phase from distance travelled, reach from horizontal speed, idle breathing at rest) that roll onto their side when dead; Field Marauders, Mirefin Lurkers, Redbrand Bandits, Garrick (the elite template, larger through its box and a darker cloak) and the guard, vendor, quest giver and spirit healer are compact humanoids posed by `poseFor` that lie flat when dead. Each model fits its template's collision box (NPCs the 0.6 m × 1.8 m character box) within 10% and draws at most 24 nodes with its target ring. The disposition colour stays an accent (animal eyes and collar, humanoid clothing) and corpses keep the shade; unknown templates fall back to the placeholder box.
- **Later presentation**: selection circles beyond the target ring and nameplates, and native client parity (step 13). Simple spell visuals on existing renderer nodes landed with step 8b.

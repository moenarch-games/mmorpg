# Ranger assets in both clients

The accepted default remains the Dark archer. Both clients acquire only the default character and equipment they draw from asset-tooling's rolling [`humanoid-ranger-male` release](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/asset-tooling/releases/tag/humanoid-ranger-male), using its `asset-downloads-v1` index. Other skin, outfit and LOD variants are available upstream but are not selected by this update.

```sh
scripts/fetch-bundles.sh
```

The Ranger index, `export.ref.json`, selected glTF/bin files, shared KTX2 textures, attachment JSON and shared clips are retained together under the ignored `web/public/characters/humanoid.ranger.male/`. The Greyhaven inn keeps its existing release and acquisition path. Acquisition never fetches the Ranger review tar.

The rolling release is read at whatever it currently holds, unpinned (#507). Acquisition first downloads the live release's `export.ref.json` and keeps the local copy while it holds that export and every selected entry; after an owner upload it fetches the selected entries again, so a new upload never needs a consumer re-pin. When the release cannot be reached, a complete local copy is kept. To keep a comparison input across uploads, preserve it outside that directory. Acquisition fails only when the live index lacks an entry the game selects. Source and license information is maintained in [asset-tooling's humanoid source documentation](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/asset-tooling/blob/main/docs/humanoid-sources.md); `export.ref.json` identifies the corresponding bundle. `scripts/prepare-ranger-native.sh` prepares every tintable hairstyle the fetched index offers.

Tanner Hilda Brook is drawn on the female figure (#493), asset-tooling's rolling [`humanoid-ranger-female` release](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/asset-tooling/releases/tag/humanoid-ranger-female) (#378). The same acquisition fetches her default entry (sockets, clips and appearance), her `outfit.tanner.skin.light` variant, the `armor.tanner` belt and every tintable hairstyle into the ignored `web/public/characters/humanoid.ranger.female/`. It is read unpinned like the male release. `imported::Figure` names the two figures and `Resident::figure` puts Hilda on the female. Both clients load each figure as its own bundle, with its own skeleton, clips, sockets and `humanoid-appearance`. The female bundle has no headwear, beards, painted head skins or variety options, so her look simply has none. `presets::figure_appearance` adds the enemy presets to the male figure only.

## Browser

```sh
cd web
bun run dev
```

Open `/mmorpg/ranger-loader.html` for the small loading example, or `/mmorpg/` for the game. The example calls the same `QuaterniusUnits` loader and `ThreeSceneRenderer` as the game, loads the default character with bow and quiver, and plays `Walk_Loop` from the shared animation library. A successful load reports the clip count; a failed load reports an error and details remain in the console.

With a running server and the existing Playwright setup, check that same example with `python3 scripts/smoke-ranger-loader.py http://localhost:5173/mmorpg/ranger-loader.html` (optional `--screenshot <path>`).

The production loader uses Three.js `GLTFLoader`, `MeshoptDecoder`, `KTX2Loader`, `SkeletonUtils.clone` and `AnimationMixer`. KTX2 support is detected against the actual renderer that draws the scene. Vite copies the Basis JS/WASM transcoder from the pinned Three.js package. Relative glTF resources remain beside the index. The animation-only GLB is loaded once; its tracks bind to each cloned character's named joints.

The named Greyhaven residents wear their authored `humanoid-appearance` presets (#418). The four Greyhaven Guards share `resident.guard`, but each guard wears his own look that stays the same between frames and sessions (asset-tooling #380). The shared presenter marks them `Resident::varied` and seeds each look with the guard's NPC id. `presets::vary_preset` in `mmorpg-presentation` (the browser calls it through `mmorpg-wasm`) keeps the preset's uniform, faction colour and sword, and draws the rest from the bundle's own options: skin, hairstyle, hair tint (the beard and brows take the same tint), facial hair and face features. It also picks an age, a build, a height and a skin tone when the bundle offers `ages`, `builds`, `heights` and `skinTones`. Acquisition therefore fetches the uniform in both skins and every hairstyle's tintable `hair-1` variant. A guard wears the plain preset until his own look has loaded, or if it fails to load. When the bundle has painted head skins (`headSkins`), half the varied men also show stubble, and both clients put the `skin.head` texture of the man's skin, his age's painted skin (`ages[].skin`) and, with stubble, his hair tint's `stubbleShades` shade on the head material before the skin tone multiplies it. A man with no matching texture (the plain adult without stubble) keeps the authored skin. Acquisition fetches `skin.head` when the release has it.

Loose gear sways a little (Atlas 6.6.39, #498). Items stowed on the hanging sockets (`back.quiver`, `back.bow.stowed`, `back.stowed`, `hip_l.stowed`: the Ranger's quiver and bow) swing behind the body and settle when it starts, stops, sidesteps or turns. `units::sway` in `mmorpg-presentation` runs one damped spring per unit from its presented root motion, and both clients turn those items about their grip by the result (`ImportedUnit::swing`). The settings: about 2.2 Hz with a 0.45 damping ratio, at most about 10° either way, full detail within 25 m of the viewer, fading out by 35 m. Farther away the gear rests as authored and the spring does no work. Reduced motion turns the sway off. A unit that teleports more than 2.5 m in a frame, comes back after an interest gap or changes loadout, preset or outfit starts again from rest. Hair, beards and cloth (robes, skirts, aprons, mantles) stay skinned as authored, because the bundle has no accessory bones to swing them by (asset-tooling #391).

The humanoid enemies wear the Ranger too (#39), in presets the game authors over the bundle's own outfits: `presets::enemy_presets` in `mmorpg-presentation`, which both clients read, keyed by `Resident::preset` (`enemy.*`). Field Marauders wear the farmer's smock with long hair and a dagger. Redbrand Bandits wear the soldier's gambeson in Redbrand red, bareheaded, with a sword. Redbrand Crossbowmen wear the same uniform with the kettle hat and carry a crossbow (#607, below). Redbrand Brutes wear the knight's plate in red under the Ironcrest helm, with sword and shield. Garrick Redbrand wears the plate bareheaded in a deeper crimson, with long black hair and a full beard. The rank and file vary like the guards, each seeded by its creature id; Garrick keeps his authored look. The models scale to each template's collision height, and bodies and hitboxes stay as they are. Acquisition also fetches the knight and farmer outfits in both skins.

Swimmers wade, tread, stroke, dive and surface with authored clips (Atlas 6.6.41, #499). The zone's water mode at the projected position (`WaterMode::of_player`) picks them in `imported::Playback`, never the rendered water: `Wade_Fwd_Loop` in wading depth, `Swim_Idle_Loop` treading, `Swim_Fwd_Loop` travelling, `Swim_Down_Loop` and `Swim_Up_Loop` from 0.4 m/s of vertical travel, and the one-shots `Swim_Enter` and `Swim_Exit` on reaching or leaving swimming depth. Strokes keep pace with the projected travel (1.8 m/s plays a clip at its authored rate). A swimmer never jumps, falls or lands, and a strike while swimming stays on the upper body. Land↔water changes crossfade over at least 0.3 s. `units::swim` leans the drawn body forward about its chest as it travels, 70° at the surface and level when submerged, up to 50° further head-down into a dive and 80° head-up out of one, and turns it at most 45° toward a sideways travel. Treading, a backward paddle and wading stay upright, and the lean eases in and out over about a quarter second. Both clients draw the leaned root (`ImportedUnit::root`; the page's `pitch` and `yaw`), and the projected position never moves. The bundle doesn't have these clips yet (asset-tooling #393). Until it does, each missing clip falls back in place to the nearest land clip: the walk for wading and strokes (leaned, it reads as a kick), the idle for treading, and no entry or exit transition.

Climbers and mantlers move with the authored traversal clips (Atlas 6.6.43, #500, asset-tooling #416). The zone projects what a climber holds (a rock or timber face, a ladder or a rope) and the height class of a mantled ledge (low 0.2–1 m, mid 1–1.6 m, high 1.6–2.2 m), and `imported::Playback` picks the clips from that. On a face, `Climb_Up/Down_Loop` plays when the body moves up or down at 0.15 m/s or more, otherwise `Climb_Left/Right_Loop` when it moves sideways, and `Climb_Idle_Loop` when it is still. Ladders and ropes play `Ladder_*` and `Rope_*` up, down or idle. Each loop's rate is the projected speed divided by the clip's `climbSpeedMetersPerSecond`, clamped to 0.5–1.6. A mantle from the ground plays `Mantle_Low/Mid/High` by the ledge class. A mantle that starts while climbing plays `Climb_TopOut`. Letting go of a face plays `Climb_LetGo` over the fall. A mantle clip is paced to end about when the zone's mantle does, at 1–1.6× its authored rate. It keeps playing after the zone's mantle ends, until the unit moves. The clips are in place and the zone moves the capsule. Through a mantle or top-out, `units::traversal::Align` holds the drawn root on the clip's `travelSamples` path from where the mantle began. The drawn root only ever trails the projected body, by at most 0.6 m up or toward the ledge, and it settles back onto the body over 0.2 s. This keeps the hands near the edge when the clip's `contacts` plant them. Both clients play each clip's `secondaryPose` as rotation channels of the `skirt_*_01` and `cape_01` chains, on every outfit that has them. No cloth spring exists yet (#498 sways only hanging gear), so the authored swing plays as it is. No `Grip_*` overlay plays while climbing. Before the bundle had these clips, climbers fell back in place to the idle and walk clips. A bundle that lacks a clip still falls back that way. Review with `?scenario=mantle-low`, `mantle-mid`, `mantle-high` or `climb`.

The Warden's sword-and-shield clips and the Redbrand specials (#582, asset-tooling #447) are picked in the shared presenter, so both clients play them:

- **Slashes.** A directed Heroic Strike plays `Sword_Slash_{High,Mid,Low}_{L,C,R}` by the aim the zone's `StrikeSwept` event carries. Its local wind-up shows the slash toward the aim being held. An ordinary swing (the free-aim swing of #517, and a sword-bearing Redbrand's attack) picks its slash on the client from the projection; no wire field was added. The side is where the target stands from the swinger's facing (more than 15° off is left or right), and the height is the band (`mmorpg_core::lane_of`) of the target's middle above the swinger's feet, so a wolf takes a low slash. A swing that covers nobody slashes level, forehand and backhand in turn. The slashes and the undirected `Sword_Heroic` play on the upper body only.
- **Timing.** A local wind-up holds at 0.18 s into a slash, and at 0.6 of the `humanoid-clips` `contact.seconds` of any other strike that has one. The Redbrand whirl (ability 24) and overhead smash (25) wind up the same way through their cast and hold there; the zone's resolved strike swings on from the hold, whole body and in place.
- **Cleave** plays `Sword_Cleave` with footwork.
- **Charge** plays `Charge_Run_Loop` (rate: speed ÷ 9 m/s, clamped to 0.8–1.6) while the zone carries the charger. Once the charger slows below 4 m/s after 0.2 s, or after 1.1 s, it plays `Charge_Impact` on the whole body.
- **Guard.** The viewer's protecting guard raises `Shield_Guard_{High,Mid,Low}_Loop` by its projected lane on the upper body. The legs keep `Idle_Shield_Loop`.
- **Stun.** A stunned unit plays `Stun_Loop`. The client knows the viewer's and its target's stuns from their projected auras, and anyone else's from the stun auras it saw applied.
- **Respawn.** A unit that comes back from death plays `Respawn_Rise`.

A bundle without these clips falls back to what played before: `Sword_Attack`, `Idle_Shield_Loop`, the idle and the shared casts. Review with `?scenario=warden-slashes`, `warden-guards`, `warden-charge`, `warden-cleave`, `warden-stun`, `warden-respawn`, `redbrand-whirl` or `redbrand-smash`.

The Rogue's dagger, throw, flourish and bandage clips (#603, asset-tooling #418) are picked in the shared presenter too (`Kind::dagger_strike`), and all of them play on the upper body only:

- **Two daggers.** The Rogue carries a blackened dagger in each hand (`Loadout::TwinDaggers`, `hand_r.weapon` and `hand_l.weapon`), as the clips are authored. Dual-wielding awaits the owner's confirmation on #603. `imported::ROGUE_LOADOUT` is the one switch: `Loadout::Dagger` gives back the single dagger. The field marauders keep one dagger.
- **Stance and auto-attack.** A standing dagger holder (the Rogue and the marauders) plays `Dagger_Idle_Loop` over the idle legs. Its auto-attack plays `Dagger_Stab_R`. With two daggers it alternates `Dagger_Stab_R` and `Dagger_Slash_L`, counted per unit on the client.
- **Abilities.** Sinister Strike (30) plays `Dagger_Sinister`, Poisoned Blade (31) `Dagger_Poison` and Gouge (32) `Dagger_Gouge`. Adrenaline Rush (33) and the Ranger's Rapid Fire (8) play `Flourish_Haste`. Throwing Knife (42) plays `Throw_Knife`, and Venom Flask (43) and Blasting Bomb (44) play `Throw_Lob`. The local wind-ups hold at 0.6 of each clip's `contact.seconds`, which for a throw is its release.
- **Throws.** At the clip's `drawSeconds` the right dagger moves to `hip_r.stowed`, and the knife, flask or bomb appears in `hand_r.weapon` with `Grip_Knife_Pinch_R` or `Grip_Flask_R`. At `releaseSeconds` it leaves the hand, and the dagger comes back when the clip ends (`imported::Carry`). The bundle has no throwable items, so both clients draw placeholders in the projectile's colours (`UnitPresenter::throwable_draws`). The zone's projectile still appears when the zone accepts the throw: nothing on the wire ties a projectile to its thrower.
- **Bandage.** A Redbrand's Crude Bandage (14) loops `Bandage_Self` through its 3 s cast. A dagger holder would sheathe both daggers at `hip_l.stowed` and `hip_r.stowed` for it; the bandits keep their swords.

A bundle without these clips falls back to what played before: the slashes or `Sword_Attack`, the idle arms, the bow release for Rapid Fire and the spell hold for the bandage. Without `hip_r.stowed` the dagger stays in hand. Review with `?scenario=rogue-daggers`, `rogue-throws` or `redbrand-bandage`.

The ranged clips (#607, asset-tooling #419) are picked in the shared presenter too, and every one plays on the upper body only:

- **Shots on the move.** Whenever a clip of the full-body bow cycle would play on the upper body alone, it plays its hips-square twin instead (`Bow_Aim_Upper_Loop`, `Bow_Draw_Upper`, `Bow_Hold_Upper_Loop`, `Bow_Release_Upper`, `Bow_Recovery_Upper`, `Bow_Cancel_Upper`; `BowClips::upper_of`). That covers every shot taken walking, running or strafing (Aimed Shot, Serpent Sting, Concussive Shot, Piercing Shot, Ricochet Shot and the free-aim shots of #517), a shot that starts standing and goes on moving, and the local aim wind-up, which never moves the legs. A shot standing still keeps the full-body cycle. The twins carry the cycle's `bow` object, so the nock follows their `drawMeters` as before (#563).
- **Rapid Fire.** While Rapid Fire's haste lasts, every bow shot plays `Bow_Rapid_Release` (0.33 s, no recovery): it starts and ends on the set string, so shots chain back to back. The client knows the viewer's haste from its projected auras, and anyone else's from the haste auras it saw applied. Using Rapid Fire still plays `Flourish_Haste`.
- **Power draw.** Aimed Shot and Piercing Shot loose with `Bow_Release_Upper` and recover with `Bow_Recovery_Upper`. Their local wind-up is `Bow_Power_Draw`, held at its end until the zone answers. Both abilities are instant, so the draw shows only for that wait: about a round trip online, and not at all against the browser's local zone, which answers within the frame.
- **Crossbowmen.** A Redbrand Crossbowman holds `equipment.crossbow` on `hand_r.crossbow` (`Loadout::Crossbow`), with `Grip_Crossbow_R` and `Grip_Crossbow_L`. It plays `Crossbow_Idle_Loop` out of combat and on the move, and `Crossbow_Aim_Loop` standing in combat (the projected `in_combat` flag) and through Crossbow Bolt's 1 s cast. Each shot, its 2.2 s swing and Crossbow Bolt alike, plays `Crossbow_Fire` (0.4 s) and then `Crossbow_Reload` (1.6 s).
- **Reload.** Over the reload's `crankSeconds` the left hand plays `Grip_Crank_L`. Over `leftHandSeconds` the crossbow rides `hand_l` where it was in the world when the hand-off began, and the right hand lets go. The arrow scaled to 0.35 m (`imported::BOLT_SCALE`) stands in for the bolt, since the bundle has no bolt item. It rides `hand_r.arrow` from `boltDrawnSeconds`, lies in the groove from `reloadedSeconds` (`imported::BOLT_GROOVE`, where the hand lays it, lifted 1.5 cm out of the stock) and leaves at the fire's `crossbow.releaseSeconds` (`imported::CrossbowCarry`). The native client measures the hand-off from the reload's pose; the browser reparents the crossbow with three.js's `attach`.

A bundle without these clips falls back to what played before: the full-body cycle on the upper body, the bow release, the aim wind-up and the idle arms. Review with `?scenario=ranger-moving-shots` (walk or strafe and shoot), `ranger-rapid-fire`, `ranger-power-draw` or `redbrand-crossbowman`.

Held items close the hand round them (#503, asset-tooling #285). The bundle's `Grip_*` clips are static finger poses. `imported::Playback` layers the one for each held item over whatever body clip plays, as a `fingers_l` or `fingers_r` layer (`Mask::Fingers`). While a pose has such a layer, only that layer drives that hand's 15 finger and thumb joints, in both clients. This holds even over the clips that key the fingers, such as the strafes, the swims and the sword clips. `Loadout::hands` names what each hand holds:

| Item | Socket | Grip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arming sword, blackened dagger, flanged mace | `hand_r.weapon` | `Grip_Weapon_R` |
| The Rogue's second dagger (#603) | `hand_l.weapon` | `Grip_Weapon_L` |
| Heater shield | `hand_l.shield` | `Grip_Shield_L` |
| Tome | `hand_l.shield` | `Grip_Book_L` |
| Bone, crystal and gnarled staffs | `hand_r.staff` | `Grip_Staff_R` |
| The Crossbowmen's crossbow (#607) | `hand_r.crossbow` | `Grip_Crossbow_R` and `Grip_Crossbow_L` (`Grip_Crank_L` while winding) |

Players, the guards, the Redbrands and the marauders all grip this way. Empty hands grip nothing. The Ranger's bow is the one exception: it comes to hand only through the bow clips, which curl their own fingers, so `Grip_Bow_L` never plays. The grips let go over the upper body's blend while the zone projects a climb or a mantle, and stay off until a mantle or let-go clip plays out. A bundle without a grip clip leaves that hand open. Review with `?scenario=grips`: a Warden stands 3 m from a Greyhaven guard, facing him. For the other classes, use `?scenario=action-lab&class=<class>`.

## Native

After acquisition, explicitly prepare the compatible native input:

```sh
scripts/prepare-ranger-native.sh
cargo run -p mmorpg-client --locked
```

Preparation requires Bun. It acquires the pinned asset-tooling processor into the user cache and, on Linux x86_64 when needed, Khronos KTX-Software 4.4.2. On other platforms install KTX-Software and set `ASSET_TOOLING_KTX` to its `ktx` CLI. `ASSET_TOOLING_ROOT` can select an existing compatible processor checkout. The processor uses glTF Transform and meshoptimizer to decode geometry and KTX-Software to transcode textures into ordinary PNGs embedded in GLB. These are prepared inputs, not another game-local decoder.

The command prepares the entries the native client draws: the character, the class weapons and starter outfits, and the residents' and enemies' outfit variants, armour, beards, straw hat and tintable hairstyles, and the painted head skins (`skin.head`) when the release has them. For the female figure it prepares Hilda's tanner outfit, its belt and her buns (#493). It writes self-contained GLBs and one separate decoded animation library per figure into each figure's ignored `native/` subdirectory. Repeat preparation after deliberately changing the acquired input. The native build embeds every prepared GLB and the original sockets descriptor; it performs no downloads. The standard `gltf` importer reads the models and library, maps animation targets to character joints by name, and uses the existing native pose/skinning path.

The native client draws the named residents and the humanoid enemies on the Ranger in the same shared presets and seeded variations as the browser, so a guard or bandit is the same man in both clients. It uses the outfit variant, armour less `hiddenArmor`, beards and hair in the preset's tint, headwear, faction colour, skin tone and height. It bakes the face, age and build morph targets into each look once (the face on `face.nodes`; age and build on the figure, plates, beards, hair and headwear, each mesh taking the targets it has), and the class starter outfits wear their preset's face. The painted head skin is sampled at the head's vertices, as every native texture is, so fine wrinkles show less than in the browser. If a preset's prepared entries are missing, the residents and enemies keep their procedural models.

The processing contract and producer commands are documented in [asset-tooling's release-download guide](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/asset-tooling/blob/main/docs/release-downloads.md). Acquisition, asset preparation, runtime presentation and gameplay remain separate owners.
